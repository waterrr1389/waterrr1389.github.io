The last time I blogged seriously was many years ago. Since then my writing scattered across platforms: short thoughts went to microblogs, long pieces to walled-garden apps, code snippets stayed in private repos. There were more distribution channels than ever, yet when I tried to find my old writing, it was spread everywhere — and some platforms had already shut down.

## Owning a corner

The difference between a blog and a platform is ownership. Platform content serves the platform's distribution logic: headlines chase clicks, length chases completion rates, timing chases traffic peaks. A blog answers only to its author. Nobody blames you for writing short, and nobody throttles you for writing niche.

Technically it has never been easier, either. A static site generator plus free hosting costs almost nothing per year. This site is built exactly that way.

## Writing is thinking

The other reason to start again: many ideas only count as thought-through once written down. Things that feel like "I get it" in my head reveal their gaps the moment I try to shape them into coherent sentences. In that sense a blog is a thinking tool first and a publishing channel second.

So no promises on frequency, and no promises on focus. I will write whatever comes to mind, for as long as it lasts.
